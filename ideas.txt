================================================================================
ANIMATION
================================================================================

Already built (for reference, don't redo):
  flame reacts to scroll speed · rocket leans into travel · ignition ring on
  open, reverse ring on close · category count-up · envelope flap · deco
  parallax · cursor-following orbit in the hero · year scramble on hover ·
  rail separator charges to the rocket · turning gears on every divider

--- STRONGEST UNBUILT ---

  * Gears turn with scroll velocity.
    They spin at a fixed rate now. Driving them from the same signal the flame
    uses would tie the whole page to one mechanical system. Cheapest big win
    on this list — the signal already exists.

  * Gears counter-rotate down the page.
    Alternate direction per divider so consecutive gears read as a gear train.

  * Rail separator's bright band already tracks the rocket — next step is the
    rail planets rotating slowly, each at its own rate. They're planets.

--- ENTRY / CARD INTERACTIONS ---

  * Tags populate sequentially when an entry opens, like a spec sheet filling.
  * Entry images slide in slightly offset as the body expands.
  * Open entries mark their group head: "2 of 6 open".
  * Entry opens like a drawer — subtle scale-x on the left edge.
  * Card descriptions reveal staggered on scroll-in.
  * The "Open" arrow becomes a rocket on hover.
  * Cards tilt 2–3° toward the cursor. Physical, easy to overdo.

--- HERO ---

  * Cursor orbit reacts to what's under it — tightens over the name, opens
    over empty photo.
  * Ken Burns drift on the hero photo, 40s cycle, independent of parallax.
  * Scroll cue draws a trajectory arc instead of bobbing.
  * Credential chips deal in left-to-right after the name settles.

--- COUNTDOWN / MISSION CLOCK ---

  * Below T+010 the readout goes red-orange and the flame idles hot.
  * At T+000 the rocket gives one small bob and the flame flares.
  * Mission clock digits flip individually, split-flap style (reuse the year
    scramble machinery).

--- STRUCTURAL MOTION ---

  * Section titles wipe in behind their own divider as it draws left-to-right.
  * Photo strip reverses direction with scroll direction.
  * Nav underline travels between items rather than fading independently.
  * Waves respond to scroll velocity — choppier when you scroll fast.
  * Timeline spine "draws" downward as you scroll through it.

--- EASTER EGGS ---

  * Typing "launch" anywhere runs T+010 → T+000, then a rocket crosses the
    viewport diagonally.
  * Konami code turns every decoration warm for a few seconds.
  * Clicking the hero name three times animates the pronunciation line.

--- RULE FOR ANY NEW ANIMATION ---

  Never animate from invisible. The scroll-reveal fades were removed because
  content hidden until scrolled past fails anyone who lands and just looks.
  Animate from a visible resting state.
  Every JS effect needs its own `if(reduced) return` — the CSS kill switch
  can't stop JavaScript writing inline styles.

STRUCTURE

--- DECIDED AGAINST (for now) ---

  * A blog. An abandoned blog dated eighteen months ago actively damages a
    portfolio. Revisit only with three posts already written.
    Middle ground if tempted: a single undated "Notes" page. No schedule
    implied, so nothing goes stale.

  * Splitting into multiple pages. A portfolio's job is to be finished in one
    sitting; every click is a place someone leaves.
    The boundary that WOULD justify a second page: content people arrive at
    directly rather than encounter while reading about you.
      - project detail pages -> no, nobody links to them
      - photo album          -> yes, eventually. A gallery is a destination.
      - blog                 -> yes, when it exists
    When you do: a second .html that copies the <style> block wholesale. Two
    files duplicating CSS beats one file plus a build step.

--- OPEN QUESTIONS ---

  * Which four go in Highlights. Currently JPL / MIT / POLANA / EYP.
    The case against: none of them is a rocket, and the site's argument is
    "things that survive being set on fire". Two of four on leadership proves
    one point twice.
    Suggested: JPL / HELIOS / MIT / POLANA, with EYP prominent in the
    Leadership group instead. Decide before cropping photos.

  * Merging Education and Experience in the rail. They're one object already —
    seven rail items is at the practical ceiling, and this is the mergeable pair.

  * Category cards vs mosaic cards still look alike. Fixed partly (filled pill
    vs outlined pill). Remaining options: make category cards a horizontal
    strip of short bars, or fold them into the section head as inline links.

  * "Everything" leads with Rocketry and ends with Leadership & teaching —

  * Hull's School may not earn a timeline row on a page led by MIT and ETH.

TWO BIG IDEAS

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BIG IDEA 1 — THE MISSION LOG
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT
  A dated, append-only log of short entries. Two or three sentences each, in
  mono, timestamped. Not a blog — no titles, no posts, no comments, no
  obligation to be interesting.

    T+ 2026.03.14   Third valve seat iteration. Leaks at 40 bar, holds at 35.
                    The problem is the seal geometry, not the material.
    T+ 2026.03.02   First full thesis simulation ran overnight. Twelve hours.
                    Result was wrong but wrong in a way that makes sense.

WHY IT'S DIFFERENT FROM A BLOG
  A blog promises articles. A log promises nothing — you write when something
  happened. An abandoned blog looks like failure; a log with a three-month gap
  just means nothing happened worth writing down. That asymmetry is the whole
  point, and it's why this survives where a blog wouldn't.

WHY IT FITS THIS SITE SPECIFICALLY
  The page already has a mission clock, gears, a progress rail, spec-sheet
  metadata. It's dressed as an engineering document and doesn't yet contain
  anything an engineering document actually holds. This is that thing.

WHAT IT ADDS THAT NOTHING ELSE DOES
  Every other section is finished work, past tense, polished. This is work in
  progress, present tense, uncertain. It's the only thing on the site that
  could show someone HOW you think rather than what you've completed — which
  is what a research group or an employer is actually trying to find out.

WHERE
  Its own page (a log is a destination), linked from the footer and the About
  section. Newest first, no pagination, no tags. One HTML file you append to.

COST
  A second .html file copying the <style> block. Nothing else.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
BIG IDEA 2 — ANNOTATED HARDWARE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT
  One photograph, full-bleed, of something you actually built — a valve
  assembly, the HELIOS test stand, the engine. Numbered hotspots on it. Click
  or hover a number and a short note explains that part: what it does, what
  went wrong with it, what you'd change.

    (1)  Cryogenic ball valve — 3D printed. The seat is the hard part;
         everything else is geometry.
    (2)  Propellant line. Routed three times before this one survived a test.
    (3)  This bracket is the only part that never failed. I have no idea why.

WHY
  Right now the site TELLS people you're a hardware engineer. This is the only
  idea that SHOWS it. A photo with a caption says "I was there". A photo you
  can take apart says "I know what every part of this does and I remember what
  went wrong with each one" — which is a completely different claim, and one
  almost nobody can fake.

WHY IT SUITS YOU
  Your differentiator is depth on physical systems that fail. This is the only
  format that makes depth visible without making the reader read an essay. And
  it turns the site's weakest asset — static placeholder photos — into its
  strongest one.

WHERE
  Between Highlights and Everything, as its own band. One piece of hardware.
  Not a gallery — one object, done properly.

TECHNICAL SHAPE
  Absolutely-positioned buttons over a photo, percentage coordinates so they
  scale. Click opens a small panel. On mobile the hotspots become a numbered
  list under the image. Roughly the complexity of the mosaic — the site already
  does everything this needs.

RISK
  Needs one genuinely good, well-lit photograph of one thing. If that photo
  doesn't exist, this doesn't work. Worth taking one deliberately.
