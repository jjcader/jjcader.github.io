I want to push the frontier of space technology, and I'd quite like to be holding a wrench while it happens.

Most of my engineering has been on hardware that is actively trying to fail: cryogenic valves, liquid rocket engines, test stands that have to survive being fired. At ARIS I went from designing propellant supply systems to leading the technical advisory team for HERMES. At NASA JPL I swapped hardware for signal processing on the Lunar Crater Radio Telescope, which was a very different kind of hard. Right now I'm at MIT writing my master's thesis.

Alongside the engineering I've spent years building organisations and teaching — co-founding POLANA, chairing debates across Europe, mentoring first-years at ETH. Turns out getting fifteen people to agree on a valve schedule is the same skill as chairing a committee.
